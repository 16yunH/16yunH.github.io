Research Interests

I am interested in robot learning, world models, and vision-language-action policies. My current questions concern how execution feedback and corrective data help a policy recover from failures, preserve required task progress, and generalize to unseen failure states. I study these questions through imitation learning, policy adaptation, and controlled closed-loop evaluation, including whether predicted futures provide useful information beyond observed execution history.

Ongoing research

Execution Feedback and Recovery for Robotic Manipulation

Advised by Wenchao Ding

Developing execution-feedback experiments with LingBot-VA on LIBERO-Long. I use state replay and controlled interventions to distinguish changes in model outputs, the availability of feasible recovery actions, and actual closed-loop task completion.

My current research asks how task-state feedback and recovery-data adaptation can help the same policy revise actions at subtask handoffs while preserving required task progress. I am investigating the value of feedback beyond ordinary recovery training, and whether predicted futures add useful information. Preliminary successes are limited to specific states and stages; recovery on unseen failure states remains under investigation.
